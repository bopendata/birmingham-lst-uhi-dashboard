# Monthly Land Surface Temperature (LST) and Urban Heat Island (UHI) Dataset

**Location:** Birmingham, Jefferson County and Shelby County, Alabama  
**Version:** 1.0

## Overview

This interactive dashboard was developed using **QGIS2Web** to visualize monthly **Land Surface Temperature (LST)** and **Urban Heat Island (UHI)** patterns across Birmingham and the surrounding areas of Jefferson and Shelby Counties, Alabama, for the period **January 2025 through December 2025**.

The dashboard provides monthly raster datasets derived from **MODIS Land Surface Temperature observations** and allows users to explore seasonal variations in surface temperature and urban heat island intensity.

Processed raster datasets used in the dashboard are included for download.

## Processed Datasets

The processed datasets include:

- Monthly LST raster datasets (January–December 2025)
- Monthly UHI raster datasets (January–December 2025)

Each raster dataset is provided in **GeoTIFF** format.

- LST rasters contain average monthly land surface temperatures (°C).
- UHI rasters represent the difference between each pixel's LST and the regional mean LST for the corresponding month.


## Data Processing

Daily MODIS Land Surface Temperature observations were downloaded for each month and clipped to the Birmingham study area.

Monthly average LST rasters were generated by calculating the mean of all valid observations available within each month.

Urban Heat Island (UHI) intensity was calculated by comparing each pixel's LST value with the regional mean monthly LST.

Interpretation of UHI values:

- Positive values indicate areas warmer than the regional average.
- Negative values indicate relatively cooler areas.
- Values near zero represent temperatures similar to the regional mean.

## Notes

This dashboard is intended for visualization and spatial analysis of monthly land surface temperature and urban heat island patterns.

The datasets are derived from MODIS satellite observations with an approximate spatial resolution of **1 km**.

Cloud contamination and the spatial resolution of the source imagery may influence the representation of localized temperature variations.

Users requiring updated or higher-resolution datasets should consult the original data provider.
